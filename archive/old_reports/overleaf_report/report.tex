\section{Introduction}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------

We compare Random Search (RS), Grid Search (GS), Successive Halving (SH),
Hyperband (HB), and Bayesian Optimisation (BO) on two YAHPO Gym surrogate
benchmarks~\cite{pfisterer2022yahpo}: NB301 (neural architecture search,
CIFAR-10) and rbv2\_xgboost (XGBoost tuning, dataset~16).
HB achieves the best final accuracy on NB301; on rbv2\_xgboost the
multi-fidelity methods converge faster without improving final quality.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Methods}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Random Search (RS).}
RS~\cite{bergstra2012random} samples configurations uniformly at random
and evaluates each at maximum fidelity, ignoring all prior observations.

\paragraph{Grid Search (GS).}
GS discretises each continuous dimension into 5 evenly-spaced values
crossed with all categorical choices; due to the infeasible $8^{34}$
Cartesian explosion of NB301's 34-dimensional categorical space, GS
falls back to uniform random sampling of unique configurations to remain
computationally tractable.

\paragraph{Successive Halving (SH).}
SH~\cite{jamieson2016non} starts with $n{=}\lceil\eta^{s_{\max}+1}/(s_{\max}+1)\rceil$
configurations at $r_{\min}$, keeps the top $1/\eta$ fraction per round,
and promotes survivors to $r \cdot\eta$ until the highest rung below
$r_{\max}$ ($\eta{=}3$, $s_{\max}{=}\lfloor\log_\eta(r_{\max}/r_{\min})\rfloor$).

\paragraph{Hyperband (HB).}
HB~\cite{li2018hyperband} runs $s_{\max}{+}1$ SH brackets with
$(n_s{=}\lceil(B/r_{\max})\cdot\eta^s/(s{+}1)\rceil,\;
r_s{=}\max(r_{\min}, r_{\max}{\cdot}\eta^{-s}))$,
hedging against the sensitivity of any single bracket to its initial
candidate count.

\paragraph{Bayesian Optimisation (BO).}
BO~\cite{snoek2012practical} fits a GP (Mat\'{e}rn-5/2 + WhiteKernel)
to all past evaluations and selects the next configuration by maximising
Expected Improvement over 1\,000 random candidates (5 random warm-up
evaluations; GP and EI implemented from scratch in NumPy).

% ----------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Experimental Setup}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{NB301}: fidelity = epochs ($r_{\min}{=}1$, $r_{\max}{=}98$),
metric = val.\ accuracy.
\textbf{rbv2\_xgboost/16}: fidelity = training fraction, metric = accuracy.
Budget $B{=}50{\times}r_{\max}$ fidelity units for all algorithms.
Hyperparameter spaces accessed via ConfigSpace without the forbidden
sampling methods; 10 independent random seeds per algorithm.

\paragraph{NB301.}
All methods reach ${\approx}93\%$ within 500 budget units
(Figure~\ref{fig:incumbent}) but diverge afterwards.
HB achieves the highest final accuracy ($94.16{\pm}0.20$); SH is
weakest ($92.79{\pm}0.17$) because with $\eta{=}3$ its rungs top out
at $r{=}81$, never reaching $r_{\max}{=}98$, whereas HB anchors its
top bracket from $r_{\max}$ and always evaluates at full fidelity.
BO matches HB in mean ($94.08$) but is less consistent
(std~$=0.32$, Figure~\ref{fig:boxplot}): NB301's 34 categorical
hyperparameters produce a ${\approx}272$-dimensional one-hot vector,
making the GP surrogate difficult to optimise with random candidate
sampling and leading to high variance across seeds, though BO still
outperforms Random Search on average.
RS and GS perform nearly identically, as expected.

\paragraph{rbv2\_xgboost.}
BO achieves the highest final accuracy ($0.974{\pm}0.009$), but
the more interesting story is in convergence speed
(Figure~\ref{fig:incumbent}).
SH and HB reach ${\approx}0.96$ after only~10 budget units, while
RS, GS, and BO need 20--30 units to match that level.
XGBoost rankings at small training fractions correlate well with
full-data rankings, so multi-fidelity methods can cheaply discard
poor configurations early and reach a strong incumbent fast.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusions}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------

HB achieves the best final accuracy on NB301; its bracket portfolio
hedges against a poor initial candidate count, the key advantage over
a single SH bracket.
On rbv2\_xgboost, BO reaches the highest final accuracy
($0.974{\pm}0.009$), but SH and HB find a good incumbent significantly
faster, making them the practical choice when convergence speed matters.
BO shows high variance on NB301 ($\text{std}{=}0.32$) due to the
${\approx}272$-dimensional one-hot encoding making GP optimisation
difficult with random candidate sampling, yet it still outperforms
Random Search on average, confirming the surrogate is learning the
landscape despite the dimensionality challenge.
