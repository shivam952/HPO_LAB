\section{Introduction}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------

We extend our Assignment~1 comparison with BOHB~\cite{falkner2018bohb},
which combines the bracket portfolio of Hyperband~\cite{li2018hyperband}
with model-guided configuration proposal via a Random Forest
surrogate~\cite{hutter2011smac} and Expected Improvement.
We evaluate all seven algorithms across six YAHPO Gym
benchmarks~\cite{pfisterer2022yahpo} and study two surrogate
refitting strategies (per-round and per-bracket).

% ----------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Methods}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{BOHB.}
BOHB~\cite{falkner2018bohb} runs the same $s_{\max}{+}1$ Successive
Halving brackets as Hyperband.
Before each round, it attempts to fit a Random Forest surrogate
(100 trees, \texttt{min\_samples\_leaf}~=~3;
following~\cite{hutter2011smac}) on all observations collected at
the highest fidelity with at least $N_{\min}{=}\max(10,\,n_\text{hp}+1)$
data points, where $n_\text{hp}$ is the number of hyperparameters.
The next configuration is drawn by maximising Expected Improvement
(EI)~\cite{snoek2012practical} over a pool of 64 random candidates:
$\text{EI}(x)=(\mu{-}f^*)\Phi(Z)+\sigma\phi(Z)$,
$Z{=}(\mu{-}f^*)/\sigma$, where $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are the
per-candidate mean and standard deviation of tree predictions.
A fraction $\rho{=}1/3$ of configurations is always drawn uniformly
at random (Algorithm~2, Falkner et~al.), preserving the convergence
guarantee when low-fidelity rankings are misleading.

Categorical hyperparameters are \emph{label-encoded}
(each mapped to $\mathrm{index}/(|\mathrm{choices}|-1)$) rather than
one-hot encoded.
For NB301 this reduces the encoded dimension from~272 to~34, bringing
$N_{\min}$ from~273 down to~35 and allowing the surrogate to activate
early in the run.
No output transformation is applied; the RF is trained on raw
performance values, which is consistent with prior work~\cite{falkner2018bohb}
and appropriate here since YAHPO Gym metrics are bounded and
well-conditioned (accuracy in $[0,1]$ or $[0,100]$).

We compare two surrogate refitting strategies:
\textbf{BOHB-Round} fits the model once at the start of each round;
all brackets share the same snapshot.
\textbf{BOHB-Bracket} refits at the start of each bracket within a
round, so later brackets benefit from observations accumulated by
earlier ones.

\paragraph{Baselines.} Random Search (RS)~\cite{bergstra2012random},
Grid Search (GS), Successive Halving (SH)~\cite{jamieson2016non},
Hyperband (HB)~\cite{li2018hyperband}, and Bayesian Optimisation (BO)
with a GP surrogate~\cite{snoek2012practical} are as in Assignment~1.

\section{Experimental Setup}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------

Six YAHPO Gym benchmarks:
NB301/CIFAR-10 ($r_{\max}{=}98$ epochs, val.\ accuracy),
rbv2\_xgboost/16 ($r_{\max}{=}1$, acc),
rbv2\_svm/16, rbv2\_glmnet/16, rbv2\_ranger/16
($r_{\max}{=}1$, acc), and LCBench/3945 ($r_{\max}{=}51$ epochs,
val.\ accuracy).
Total budget $B{=}50{\times}r_{\max}$ fidelity units, $\eta{=}3$,
10 independent seeds per algorithm (420 runs total).

\paragraph{NB301.}
BOHB-Bracket achieves the highest mean ($94.23{\pm}0.43$), narrowly
ahead of Hyperband ($94.16{\pm}0.20$), though the difference lies
within one standard deviation.
BOHB-Round ($94.05{\pm}0.33$) is slightly below Hyperband because
the shared model is fitted with fewer observations before the run
warms up: the surrogate only activates once $N_{\min}{=}35$ points
are available at a single fidelity, which requires about a full round.
BOHB-Bracket mitigates this by refitting before each bracket, so
brackets~$s{>}0$ benefit from observations already accumulated.
The higher variance of both BOHB variants relative to Hyperband reflects
that the RF occasionally proposes a configuration that looks promising
under EI but underperforms at full fidelity, a consequence of the
limited 35-point warm-up data available on a 34-dimensional space.
SH underperforms ($92.79{\pm}0.17$) because its single bracket never
reaches $r_{\max}{=}98$ with $\eta{=}3$.

\paragraph{rbv2\_xgboost.}
BOHB-Round achieves the best mean ($0.976{\pm}0.008$), surpassing
Bayesian Optimisation ($0.974{\pm}0.009$) and Hyperband ($0.967{\pm}0.007$).
XGBoost's search space is lower-dimensional and largely continuous,
making the RF surrogate easier to fit; model-guided sampling at low
fidelity cheaply identifies promising regions before promoting to full
training fraction.
BOHB-Bracket scores slightly lower ($0.972{\pm}0.002$): the additional
refit overhead consumes budget that BOHB-Round can spend on evaluations.

\paragraph{Other benchmarks.}
On rbv2\_svm and rbv2\_ranger, Bayesian Optimisation leads, which is
expected given their smaller search spaces where a GP surrogate modelling
at full fidelity is highly effective.
On rbv2\_glmnet, Grid Search achieves the best mean, suggesting the
parameter space is small enough for uniform coverage to be competitive.
LCBench/3945 is dominated by Bayesian Optimisation ($99.62{\pm}0.01$),
with BOHB-Bracket a close second ($99.56{\pm}0.03$).
Across all six benchmarks, both BOHB variants consistently outperform
Random Search and Grid Search, and are competitive with or better than
Hyperband.

\section{Surrogate Analysis}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{figures/nb301_cifar10_surrogate_usage_ratio.pdf}
  \caption{Cumulative model-usage ratio on NB301 (median $\pm$ IQR).
           BOHB-Bracket reaches a higher model-usage ratio earlier
           because per-bracket refitting provides fresh data sooner.}
  \label{fig:usage}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:usage} plots the fraction of configurations proposed
by the RF surrogate (versus random sampling) over the course of the run
on NB301.
Both variants start at zero (no data yet) and climb toward
$1{-}\rho{=}0.67$ as observations accumulate.
BOHB-Bracket consistently exceeds BOHB-Round early in the run because
it refits with all available data before each bracket, whereas
BOHB-Round fits once per round and then freezes the model.
The convergence of both curves toward the same plateau confirms that
$\rho{=}1/3$ random exploration is maintained throughout, as required
by Algorithm~2 of Falkner et~al.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusions}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------

BOHB matches or exceeds Hyperband on every benchmark tested.
On NB301, BOHB-Bracket achieves the highest mean accuracy
($94.23{\pm}0.43$) but with greater variance, reflecting the warmup
phase of the surrogate.
On rbv2\_xgboost, BOHB-Round clearly wins, demonstrating the benefit
of model-guided sampling on a well-structured continuous space.
Per-bracket refitting (BOHB-Bracket) is preferable on
high-dimensional or structured benchmarks where earlier brackets
provide informative priors; per-round refitting (BOHB-Round) is
advantageous when the additional refitting overhead would otherwise
consume useful evaluation budget.
Label encoding (instead of one-hot) is critical: it keeps NB301's
encoded dimension at 34 rather than 272, reducing $N_{\min}$ from
273 to 35 and allowing the surrogate to activate early enough to
influence the run outcome.
